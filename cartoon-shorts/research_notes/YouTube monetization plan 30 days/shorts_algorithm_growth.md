# YouTube Shorts Algorithm and Data-Proven Growth Tactics (2025-2026)

Research date: 2026-11-07. Context: new channel, one original 60-70 s vertical cartoon 3D history/science story Short per day, 30-day sprint.

Overall caveat on sources: YouTube publishes no numeric thresholds for any Shorts metric. Nearly every "benchmark" number online comes from vendor blogs (OpusClip, Subscribr, Fluxnote, Toptal and others) that give no sample size. The strongest datasets found were the vidIQ 448k-channel study (2026), Metricool's 799,718 videos (2026), AIR Media-Tech's 18,000 channels (2026), and Paddy Galloway's 5,400 Shorts (2023). Official statements come mainly from Todd Sherman (Shorts product lead) on Creator Insider (Aug 2023) and YouTube's May 2025 "myths" communication.

## 1. Distribution signals and benchmark values

### Takeaway
Viewed vs swiped away (VVSA) and how much of the Short people watch are the levers that can be measured. The only dataset-backed thresholds are Galloway's (2023): Shorts below 60% VVSA rarely did well, top performers sat at 70-90%, and Shorts with average view duration (AVD) above 50 s averaged 4.1M views. Likes, comments and shares showed no strong relationship with views in that dataset. YouTube itself publishes no thresholds.

### Cited Findings
- **Official mechanism: explore/exploit.** A new Short is shown to a small seed audience and pushed wider if that group engages; otherwise it stalls. Sherman (Shorts product lead, Creator Insider) explains that most Shorts views come from the feed rather than a deliberate choice, so each Short gets tested. — [vidIQ, updated Oct 9 2026](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- **Official: separate recommendation systems.** Sherman: Shorts and long-form use different systems because viewing differs: "On short form, people are swiping through a feed and discovering things as they go." Watch duration affects impressions, and YouTube does not disclose the exact measurement in order to prevent gaming. — [Net Influencer, May 23 2025](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-debunks-common-myths-about-shorts-as-format-gains-traction/); [Gigazine summary of Creator Insider "YouTube Shorts Algorithm — Explained!", Aug 2023](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20230828-youtube-shorts-algorithm); original video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n3jsYK_-aRU
- **Hard data, 2023: VVSA thresholds.** Shorts under 60% VVSA "rarely performed well"; the best performers were typically 70-90%. High VVSA does not guarantee success. Sample: 5,400 Shorts, 33 channels, 3.3B views. — [Paddy Galloway thread, Apr 2023](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)
- **Hard data, 2023: watch time.** Shorts with AVD above 50 s averaged 4.1M views. Galloway's conclusion: holding viewers as long as possible matters more than length alone. — [Galloway, Apr 2023](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)
- **Hard data, 2023: engagement.** Likes, shares and comments showed "no strong relationship" with performance; more testing needed. — [Galloway, Apr 2023](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)
- **No official benchmarks.** YouTube publishes no single "good retention" or VVSA threshold. The Bytecap synthesis (updated Jul 17 2026) cites YouTube Help pages 12942217, 9313698 and 11914225 and recommends comparing against the channel's own Shorts of similar length. — [Bytecap](https://www.bytecap.io/research/youtube-shorts-retention-benchmarks)
- **Opinion (no sample): VVSA bands.** Vendor blogs repeat 70%+ viewed as "viral territory", about 50% as average, and below 30% as dead, with 75-80% as a common target. — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-analytics-metrics-viral); [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-rate)
- **Opinion (no sample), OpusClip Nov 2025:**
  - 50-60% of viewers who drop off do so in the first 3 s ("thousands of Shorts", unspecified).
  - A replay rate around 10% "meaningfully" boosts distribution.
  - Burned-in captions give 15-25% higher retention.
  - Top performers cut every 2-4 s.
  - None of this has a stated methodology. — [OpusClip](https://opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention)
- **Opinion (unsourced):** Shorts of 60+ s "usually require 80-90% retention" to perform strongly. — [Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/youtube-shorts-length)
- **Official metric change, Mar 31 2025.**
  - Public Shorts "views" now count every start or replay, with no minimum watch time.
  - The old metric was renamed "engaged views" and is available in Analytics Advanced Mode.
  - YPP eligibility and Shorts revenue sharing still run on engaged views.
  - Every loop counts as a new view. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-changes-how-shorts-views-are-counted-from-march-31/); [Digital Music News, Mar 27 2025](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2025/03/27/how-does-youtube-shorts-count-views-2025/); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/); official help page cited in coverage: support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070 (not opened directly)
- **Hard data, 2026: the feed is getting shallower.** Shorts views per post rose more than 127% from 2025 to 2026, while average view duration per Short fell about 67%, to roughly 16 s, and interactions fell 15%. Sample: 799,718 videos, 71,177 accounts. Part of the view inflation likely comes from the March 2025 counting change. — [Metricool, Jul 29 2026](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)
- **Subscribes per view as a signal.** I found no official statement that subscribes-per-view is a Shorts ranking input. The vidIQ article lists VVSA, retention, loops, engaged views and metadata, but not subscriber signals. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)

### Inferences
- For a 60-70 s Short, Galloway's ">50 s AVD averaged 4.1M views" finding means roughly 75%+ average percentage viewed (APV) on a 65 s Short is the target zone. An APV above 100% (loops) is easier on 15-30 s Shorts than on 65 s ones.
- Metricool's industry-wide AVD of about 16 s per Short shows the bar: a 65 s story Short that holds 40-50 s would be far above the 2026 average.
- Track engaged views, not public views, when judging whether a Short was pushed. Since March 2025, public views are inflated by swipes and loops.
- The twist ending can drive loops, which now count as views. Only one unsourced figure (about 10% replay rate) exists for loop impact.

### Gaps
- No official or large-sample 2025-2026 dataset gives VVSA or APV thresholds. Galloway's 2023 numbers predate both the 3-minute limit and the March 2025 view-count change.
- No Todd Beaupré statement specific to Shorts thresholds was found. His public 2025 remarks (VidCon 2025 panel) concern viewer-centric recommendation in general.
- No data was found on whether subscribes per view or shares feed Shorts ranking.

## 2. Length: 60-70 s vs under 30 s

### Takeaway
Older large-sample data (2023, before the 3-minute limit) shows longer Shorts up to 60 s averaging more views, provided they hold viewers. vidIQ's 35B-view analysis found peaks at about 13 s and about 60 s. There is no rigorous 2025-2026 study of 60-180 s Shorts. Vendor claims that under-30 s Shorts retain better are unsourced. RPM did not vary with length in 2023 data.

### Cited Findings
- **Hard data, 2023.** Most Shorts were 20-40 s. The 50-60 s bucket performed best, averaging 1.7M views; longer Shorts averaged "staggeringly" more views, with outliers at every length. Sample: 5,400 Shorts, 33 channels. — [Galloway thread, Apr 2023](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html); [VideoGen writeup](https://videogen.io/blog/decoding-the-youtube-shorts-algorithm-a-deep-dive-into-3-3-billion-views)
- **Data (secondhand).** Nate Black's analysis of about 35 billion Shorts views found Shorts of about 13 s or about 60 s performed best. — [vidIQ, 2026](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- **Unverified secondhand.** A "Vidooly 2025" study of over 2M Shorts found 40-60 s had the highest average views. — cited by [OpusClip](https://opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention) / [Bytecap length page](https://www.bytecap.io/research/best-youtube-shorts-length); the original was not located.
- **Official, 2023.** Sherman: there is "no optimal number of seconds" for impressions. — [Gigazine, Aug 2023](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20230828-youtube-shorts-algorithm)
- **Official rules.** Shorts can run up to 3 minutes since Oct 2024. Claimed music and Content ID rules can affect Shorts over 1 minute, and licensed-music clips stay capped at 60 s, so anything longer needs original or royalty-free audio. — [Bytecap length page](https://www.bytecap.io/research/best-youtube-shorts-length)
- **Opinion (survey).** In an Adobe creator survey (Mar 2026), 46% of creators said under 30 s is ideal. This is creator belief, not measured performance. — [Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/youtube-shorts-length-study)
- **Unverified.** "Social Insider 2025": short videos under 90 s average 50% retention, versus about 35% above 90 s. — cited via [search summary of Bytecap/OpusClip pages](https://www.bytecap.io/research/best-youtube-shorts-length); the original was not verified.
- **RPM, hard data, 2023.** Shorts of 10-60 s all hovered around a $0.06 RPM; length made no meaningful RPM difference. — [Galloway, Apr 2023](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)
- **RPM, official, 2025.** CEO Neal Mohan said Shorts revenue per watch-hour matches long-form in several countries, including the US. — [Net Influencer, May 2025](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-debunks-common-myths-about-shorts-as-format-gains-traction/)

### Inferences
- A 60-70 s story Short sits in the length band that historically averaged the most views, but only if it holds about 50 s of AVD.
- If retention collapses mid-story, the channel would likely do better cutting to 45-55 s than staying at 70 s.
- Revenue is shared per engaged view and is roughly length-neutral, so longer Shorts earn no more per view. Under Mohan's per-watch-hour framing, longer watch could matter at the pool level, but that has not been verified.
- Recommended in-sprint test: 5-10 Shorts at about 45 s versus 65 s on similar topics, compared on engaged views and APV, as Bytecap's channel-relative method suggests.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 dataset compares 60-90 s Shorts with under-30 s Shorts on views, engaged views or RPM.
- Primary sources for the Vidooly and Social Insider figures were not found.

## 3. Test batches, second waves, how long a Short grows

### Takeaway
YouTube officially describes seed-audience testing. Creators report delayed revivals months later, but since September 2025 multiple large creators report that Shorts older than about 30 days lose distribution sharply ("the flattening"). For a 30-day sprint, assume most views come in the first days or weeks and do not count on a long tail.

### Cited Findings
- **Official.** Seed audience first, then wider promotion if engagement is good; otherwise views stall. — [vidIQ citing Sherman / Creator Insider](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- **Creator data, Sept-Dec 2025.** Mario Joos (CEO of Stokes Twins) reported across "dozens of channels" that Shorts over 30 days old saw sudden, steep declines starting September 2025, while new uploads were unaffected. Tim Chesney showed a chart of about 2B views where evergreen Shorts "simply tanked" in September. YouTube had not commented. — [Tubefilter, Dec 5 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/12/05/youtube-shorts-algorithm-change-new-uploads/amp/)
- **Anecdotal.** A Reddit creator saw a Short with a few hundred views revive about 7 months later, reaching about 184k views. Others report old videos reviving after 6-12 months. vidIQ hosts attribute revivals to evergreen or seasonal relevance or to adjacent niche content trending, often visible as "direct or unknown" traffic spikes. — [search summaries: Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1powf0s/snapshots/2025-12-17T16%3A30%3A11.782752Z); [vidIQ "Why do videos randomly get tons of views"](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/why-do-videos-randomly-get-tons-of-views)
- **Hard data, 2026: early Shorts are lottery tickets.** vidIQ's 448,413-channel study (channels created Jan-Jun 2025, views measured at 30 days) found:
  - Median Shorts views were flat at about 1,300 from upload 1 to upload 10.
  - The best of the first 10 Shorts reached a median of 8,832, about 6.5x the first.
  - 86.5% of Shorts-first channels had at least one of the first 10 Shorts reach 1k views, and 48.0% reached 10k.
  - Per individual Short, 56.6% reached 1k and 20.9% reached 10k.
  - The study is observational; it found no causal effect of posting more beyond more chances at an outlier. — [vidIQ Research, Jul 28 2026](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-first-10-videos-study/)

### Inferences
- With 30 daily Shorts, the sprint can expect a few outliers. Using vidIQ's per-video rates as a rough guide, about 6 of 30 would reach 10k views at 30 days. That is an inference from the medians, not a guarantee.
- Because of the "flattening", the back catalog will not keep compounding the way it did before 2025. Steady daily output matters more than evergreen accumulation.

### Gaps
- There is no official YouTube statement on test-batch sizes, wave timing or the September 2025 change.
- No quantitative data was found on the typical time from upload to peak for Shorts in 2025-2026.

## 4. Posting cadence, posting time, deleting or reuploading

### Takeaway
YouTube says that neither posting time nor volume affects reach, and that deleting and reuploading flops can be flagged as spam. Data shows per-post performance peaks at about 2-4 Shorts a week, while total monthly views keep rising with volume. One per day is a reasonable compromise; 2-3 per day adds lottery tickets but no proven per-video lift.

### Cited Findings
- **Official (May 2025 myths communication; Sherman, Aug 2023).** YouTube does not factor posting time into recommendations and does not reward specific posting volumes. "The timeliness of the content is more important than the time of posting." Posting many videos does not unlock hidden features, and Sherman advises fewer, higher-quality videos. — [Net Influencer, May 2025](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-debunks-common-myths-about-shorts-as-format-gains-traction/); [Gigazine, Aug 2023](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20230828-youtube-shorts-algorithm)
- **Official: deleting and reposting.** Not recommended, because repeated deleting and reposting "may be flagged as spam"; creators should "learn from it and move forward". — [Net Influencer, May 2025](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-debunks-common-myths-about-shorts-as-format-gains-traction/); [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20230828-youtube-shorts-algorithm)
- **Hard data, 2026.** Per post, Shorts perform best at 2-4 uploads a week, but monthly totals have "no real ceiling": more uploads generally mean more monthly views. Brands raised Shorts frequency by more than 20% year over year. Most Shorts go up between 2 and 6 p.m. Sample: 799,718 videos. — [Metricool, Jul 2026](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)
- **Hard data, 2024 (cross-platform).** The average Short got 646.89 views, and large YouTube accounts posted about 0.66 Shorts a day. — [Metricool newsletter/2024 study, via search summary](https://newsletter.metricool.com/p/still-trying-to-figure-out-youtube)
- **Hard data, 2026.** Channels with at least 200 Shorts saw consistent view growth over time (Nate Black, about 35B views). — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- **Hard data, 2026.** Median uploads to reach 1,000 subscribers: 90 for mostly-Shorts channels, 46 for mostly long-form, and 108 for mixed channels (200,000 channels created in 2025 that reached 1k subs). — [vidIQ "Fastest way to 1k subs in 2026", May 4 2026](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/the-fastest-way-to-1k-subs-in-2026)
- **Opinion (no methodology).** Claims such as "daily posting cuts per-Short views by 40%" are unsupported. — [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-shorts-posting-frequency-2026), flagged as unreliable.
- **Opinion (marketing).** Reposting top Shorts after 6+ months with changes is recommended, but this is not a documented mechanism and conflicts with YouTube's spam warning. — [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-shorts-viral-strategy-2026)

### Inferences
- One Short a day for 30 days means 30 Shorts. vidIQ's median of 90 Shorts to reach 1k subs suggests a typical Shorts-only channel would not hit 1k subscribers in 30 days. An outlier hit is the realistic route.
- Posting time can be chosen for operational convenience. Aligning with the audience's active hours (Studio > Audience tab) costs nothing, but YouTube says it is not a ranking factor.
- Do not delete flops. Make the remake better and different instead of reuploading the same file.

### Gaps
- No controlled study compares 1/day with 2-3/day for brand-new Shorts channels.
- No data was found on whether consistency (same time each day) matters.

## 5. Titles, hashtags, descriptions, first frame, captions

### Takeaway
Most Shorts views come from the feed, where titles and thumbnails are barely seen, so metadata mainly helps classification and the search and non-feed surfaces. Data on hashtags is weak and mixed; #Shorts is not needed. The first frame and first seconds matter far more than metadata.

### Cited Findings
- **Hard data, 2026: traffic mix.** More than 61% of organic Shorts views came from the Shorts feed, 11% from subscribers, 9% from YouTube search, about 5.8% from related videos, about 5% from playlists and about 3% from direct channel visits. — [Metricool, 799,718 videos, Jul 2026](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)
- **Hard data, 2026: hashtags.** Shorts with hashtags had about 10% fewer views but about 9% higher engagement. Metricool still advises 1-5 relevant hashtags. — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)
- **Official/vendor.** Titles and descriptions help YouTube understand a Short, especially for Search and non-feed surfaces. Hashtags are optional, and #Shorts is not needed. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- **Official.** No custom thumbnails for Shorts in the feed, because "most thumbnails are never seen". Creators pick a frame instead, which shows in search, the channel page and related placements. — [Net Influencer, May 2025](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-debunks-common-myths-about-shorts-as-format-gains-traction/)
- **Official.** Trending sounds may help discovery, but original content can perform equally well. — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-debunks-common-myths-about-shorts-as-format-gains-traction/)
- **Self-published data.** OverseerOS (18,436 videos) found 21-40 character titles had higher median views than 81-100 character titles. But among 117 channels that used both lengths, results were nearly evenly split, so the effect is largely a channel confound. — [OverseerOS, via search summary](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/best-youtube-shorts-title-length)
- **Creator tactic.** vidIQ suggests mining Studio > Advanced > Traffic source > YouTube search terms over 365 days and reusing them in Shorts titles. — [vidIQ video via Recapio](https://recapio.com/digest/this-google-update-means-more-youtube-views-interesting-by-vidiq)
- **Opinion (unsourced).** Burned-in captions give 15-25% higher retention. — [OpusClip](https://opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention)
- **Hard data, 2023.** "Make the first second punchy", because VVSA matters. — [Galloway](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)

### Inferences
- The channel's subjects (Mike the Headless Chicken, the exploding whale, the Emu War) are searchable proper nouns. Titles that contain the exact event name can capture the about 9% of views that come from search, plus Google's Shorts results.
- Search traffic is also the most likely source of long-tail views now that feed distribution "flattens" after about 30 days.
- Keep hashtags to 1-3 topical tags or none; the data does not support relying on them.

### Gaps
- No rigorous 2025-2026 study measures the effect of first-frame on-screen text or captions on VVSA.
- No data quantifies the share of Shorts views from Google Search.

## 6. Converting Shorts viewers to subscribers

### Takeaway
Shorts convert at roughly 1.7-2 subscribers per 1,000 views on average. Long-form converts about 1.3x (Galloway 2023) to 4.7x (vidIQ's own channel) better per view. Channels over 1M subscribers converted better on Shorts than on long-form, which Galloway attributes to better Shorts and better calls to action. Series formats and pinned comments lack hard data.

### Cited Findings
- **Hard data, 2023.** Shorts converted 16.9 subscribers per 10,000 views (1.69 per 1k), versus 22.7 per 10k for long-form. The pattern reversed for channels over 1M subscribers. Sample: 5,400 Shorts, 33 channels. — [Galloway](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html); [VideoGen](https://videogen.io/blog/decoding-the-youtube-shorts-algorithm-a-deep-dive-into-3-3-billion-views). Stripo republishes the same 16.9 figure as a "2025 Adam Connell" analysis, which appears to be the Galloway number recycled: [Stripo](https://research.stripo.email/youtube-benchmarks)
- **Single-channel data, 2026.** On vidIQ's own channel, Shorts needed about 505 views per subscriber (about 2 per 1k) and long-form about 106 views per subscriber (about 9.4 per 1k), making long-form about 4.7x more efficient. — [vidIQ, May 2026](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/the-fastest-way-to-1k-subs-in-2026)
- **Case data.** A channel's Shorts-to-long-form conversion rose from 0.04% to 0.5% after it configured the Shorts-to-long "bridge" (related links). — [AIR Media-Tech, May 2026](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels)
- **Official.** YouTube promotes "reply to comment with a Short" for community building. Sherman says comments can be "creative direction for the next Short", and YouTube cites whole channels built from comment-reply Shorts. — [Net Influencer, May 2025](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-debunks-common-myths-about-shorts-as-format-gains-traction/)
- **Case anecdote.** Scary Stories 666 needed 219 uploads to reach 1k subscribers, with Shorts earning a couple thousand views each. — [vidIQ](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/the-fastest-way-to-1k-subs-in-2026)

### Inferences
- At about 2 subs per 1k views, reaching 1,000 subscribers in 30 days requires about 500k Shorts views in total.
- At vidIQ's per-Short medians (about 1.3k views), 30 Shorts give about 40k views and about 80 subscribers. A mid-size outlier changes this sharply.
- A recognizable recurring series identity (same narrator character, "History's Dumbest Ideas #N" style) is the format lever most consistent with Galloway's "better Shorts plus better calls to action" explanation, though no controlled data exists.

### Gaps
- No data was found on pinned comments, end-of-Short subscribe calls, series or playlist formats, or channel branding effects on Shorts subscriber conversion.
- No large-sample 2025-2026 figure for subscribers per 1k Shorts views by niche (history or science) was found.

## 7. Using long-form alongside Shorts

### Takeaway
YouTube says Shorts do not hurt long-form. Correlational data says a moderate Shorts share helps:
- Metricool: about 5 Shorts a month correlates with about 46% more views per long-form video.
- AIR: a 25-40% Shorts share works best, and education mixed channels have 12-37% more subscribers.
- Heavy Shorts volume (35+ a month, or a Shorts share above 55%) correlates with weaker long-form.

Long-form converts subscribers better per view, and Shorts watch time does not count toward the 4,000-hour YPP path.

### Cited Findings
- **Official (May 2025).** YouTube says it has not seen Shorts negatively affect long-form; viewers engage across formats. No figures given. — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-debunks-common-myths-about-shorts-as-format-gains-traction/). A widely shared Rene Ritchie or Sherman quote ("we don't reduce long-form distribution") could not be verified in any transcript. — [Creaticalc](https://creaticalc.com/blog/do-youtube-shorts-hurt-long-form)
- **Hard data, 2026 (correlational).** Long-form views per video were:
  - 4,364 for channels posting no Shorts
  - 8,789 for channels posting about 5 Shorts a month (+46% versus the 5,985 global average)
  - back to about the no-Shorts level for channels posting 35+ Shorts a month
  - Source: [Metricool, 799,718 videos](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)
- **Hard data, 2026 (correlational): AIR Media-Tech, 18,000 English channels, Mar 2025-Mar 2026, 11 niches.**
  - Sweet spot: Shorts at 25-40% of uploads; above 55%, long-form drops. Suggested cadence: 1-2 Shorts plus 3-5 long-form a week.
  - Education: mixed channels median 113,000 subs versus 82,200 long-only (+37%, 100K-1M tier), and 395,000 versus 354,000 (+12%, 1M-10M tier). Median Shorts share in education is about 38%.
  - Science & Tech: near parity at 100K-1M, and long-only +16% at 1M-10M.
  - Channels that shifted format radically (over 30 points) did worst.
  - History was not broken out.
  - Source: [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels)
- **Hard data, 2026.** Median uploads to 1k subscribers were 46 for long-form-first channels versus 90 for Shorts-first. — [vidIQ](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/the-fastest-way-to-1k-subs-in-2026)
- **Hard data, 2026.** Shorts-first channels get early views more reliably: 86.5% had a Short reach 1k views versus 57.8% of long-form channels. Long-form views grew 51% from the 1st to the 10th upload, while Shorts stayed flat. — [vidIQ Research, 448,413 channels](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-first-10-videos-study/)
- **Anecdote: related video link.** One creator estimated about 10% click-through, with about 1,400 long-form views from about 12,500 Short views. The Spot newsletter claims 10-20% view lift for linked long-form, unverified. — [The Spot](https://spottercommunity.beehiiv.com/p/increase-views-from-related-shorts); feature mechanics: [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/?p=430655), [vidIQ Related Links](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-related-links-connect-shorts-long-videos/)
- **Academic (2024).** An arXiv paper reported a statistically significant decline in long-form views after creators began posting Shorts. Only a review of the paper was read. — [Moonlight review of arXiv 2402.18208](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/shorts-on-the-rise-assessing-the-effects-of-youtube-shorts-on-long-form-video-content)
- **Official YPP thresholds (from general knowledge, not fetched this session).** YPP requires 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 public long-form watch hours in 12 months or 10M valid public Shorts views in 90 days. Shorts watch time does not count toward the 4,000 hours. — YouTube Help support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851; verify before relying on it.

### Inferences
- For a history/science story channel, an 8-15 min weekly compilation of 5-7 Shorts' stories, or a deep dive on the best-performing topic, would:
  - add the better-converting format (vidIQ, Galloway)
  - start the 4,000-hour clock that Shorts cannot fill
  - give Shorts a related-video target
- The data is correlational. AIR and Metricool both suggest a Shorts-heavy mix (daily Shorts, roughly 85-90% of uploads) sits outside the "sweet spot" for long-form performance. The cost of adding a weekly long-form is low, but expect long-form views to start small and grow over the first 10 uploads.
- Shorts-to-long conversion is small (0.04-0.5% in AIR's case, about 10% CTR in one anecdote), so long-form should not depend on Shorts traffic alone.

### Gaps
- No causal data was found on whether Shorts views on the same channel lower long-form recommendations.
- No data on compilation-format long-form (re-cut Shorts) versus original deep dives.

## 8. Trending and seasonal topic timing, Shorts search

### Takeaway
YouTube says timeliness of content matters more than time of posting. Revived Shorts are often attributed to seasonal or evergreen relevance. Search accounts for about 9% of Shorts views, and searchable event names can draw search traffic over time.

### Cited Findings
- **Official.** "The timeliness of the content is more important than the time of posting." Quick response matters for news topics. — [Gigazine summary of Sherman, Aug 2023](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20230828-youtube-shorts-algorithm)
- **Opinion/anecdote.** Older videos revive because they become seasonally relevant or adjacent niche content trends. — [vidIQ hosts](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/why-do-videos-randomly-get-tons-of-views); [Miraflow](https://miraflow.ai/blog/old-youtube-videos-getting-views-again-2026)
- **Hard data, 2026.** YouTube search drives about 9% of organic Shorts views. — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)
- **Official scale.** Shorts average over 200B daily views (Neal Mohan, Cannes Lions, Jun 2025), up from 70B in Mar 2024. — [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/youtube-shorts-200-billion-daily-views); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-reports-200-billion-daily-views-for-shorts-format/549315/)

### Inferences
- Schedule stories on their anniversaries or around related news, and make each title contain the searchable event name. Examples:
  - The exploding whale on Nov 12 (1970)
  - The Emu War around its Nov-Dec 1932 dates
  - Mike the Headless Chicken around Sept 10 (1945)
  - The Eiffel Tower con around Eiffel or Paris news

### Gaps
- No quantitative data was found on the Shorts view uplift from anniversary or trend timing, or on Google Trends correlation for Shorts.
