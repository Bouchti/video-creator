# Why the 2D-in-3D cartoon shorts go viral (research, 2026-11-05)

Data: yt-dlp metadata + auto transcripts of Ricks Clips (@RicksClips3000) shorts, plus Zack D. Films top shorts and
the trends24 world Shorts chart (Nov 5). Raw files: session scratchpad ytenv/ (rick_meta.txt, transcripts.txt).

## Ricks Clips: same look as ours, the cleanest experiment possible
- Old format (sitcom gags, 12 s clips, before Aug 2026): 50K-1M views.
- Switched to "2D cartoon people in a 3D world, strange true story" on Oct 7 2026: 25 shorts in 29 days (~1/day),
  ~260M views. Median ~8M, top 31.7M (The Ancient Jar That Warned an Empire).
- Same channel, same look, same voice; only the story type changes the result:

| Type | Examples (views) |
|---|---|
| Person + a trick/bet/warning everyone mocks, then a real mechanism pays it off | Ancient Jar 31.7M, Perfect Railroads 21.7M, Storm Oil 13.8M, Grave Bell 11.5M, Spear vs Shield 8.9M, Burglar 8.2M, Second Draw 8.2M, Winning Tickets 6.1M, Mirror horse race 5.1M |
| "Imagine you're..." in a weird real system | Japan's Job Nobody Wants 16.6M, Japan gambling window 14.1M, Idea Zone 14.2M |
| Plain "how X works" explainer, no protagonist | How Painkillers Actually Work 0.30M, The Science Behind Slot Machines 0.11M |
| Invented fiction / survival list | Every Memory Has a Price 0.47M, This Tree Hates You 0.98M |

The explainers flopped 30-70x below the channel median with the identical art style.

## The formula of the hits (from the transcripts)
1. Line 1 = a person doing something that sounds stupid, with stakes: "He bet his life savings on the slowest horse",
   "He poured cooking oil into the sea with a pirate ship behind them", "They buried a man alive with a bell on his finger".
2. Mockery / doubt escalates 3-5 beats with short in-character lines ("We're about to get boarded and you're making soup?").
3. One "Wait, what?" mystery beat in the middle.
4. "You see, ..." reveals the REAL mechanism (thermal expansion, oil calming waves, pendulum seismometer) through one
   everyday analogy (blowing on soup).
5. Ends on the twist/punchline. No question, no CTA, no summary.
- ONE story, ONE mechanism, ONE place. ~60 s (31-78), ~3.7 words/s, voice from frame 1.
- Titles are a curiosity gap, not a topic: "Why Perfect Railroads Don't Work", "What the Grave Bell Really Meant".

## Zack D. Films (29M subs, same "why X" genre, realistic 3D)
- Top: Why Dobermans Have Cropped Ears 46.6M, How Horseflies Bite You 18.6M, How To Find Tiny Things You Dropped 18.6M.
- 28-49 s, second person, visceral/useful hook, one mechanism. Median of last 60 shorts ~8M.

## Why our escalator + chip shorts got few views
- Chip: 17 facts in 74 s (transistor, NYT, sand, crystal, stencil, yellow light, tin, vacuum, mirrors, jets...).
  No protagonist, no conflict, no mystery, no payoff: exactly the "How Painkillers Actually Work" type, but denser.
- Line 1 is a dense technical claim, not a human situation. Viewers swipe in the first 2 s.
- Ends on a viewer question; the hits end on a twist.
- Escalator: same "how it works" type.
- Two uploads on a new channel; the hits come from daily posting (Ricks: 25 in 29 days).
- Art style is NOT the problem: our look was modelled on this exact channel.
- Note: "184M views in 26 days, make these with 1 Claude command" tutorials now exist, so the look alone will saturate.
  The story writing is the moat.
