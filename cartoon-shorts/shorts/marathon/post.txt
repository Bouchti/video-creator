Why the 1904 Olympic Marathon Winner Lost His Gold

DESCRIPTION:
The men's marathon at the 1904 St. Louis Olympics (30 August 1904) was run at about 90 °F on dusty dirt roads. Chief organiser James E. Sullivan allowed only one water source, a well at about the 12-mile mark, because he wanted to study "purposeful dehydration". Officials drove cars ahead of the runners and raised clouds of dust; 32 men started and only 14 finished.

Fred Lorz got cramps after about 9 miles, rode in a car and waved at runners and spectators. When the car broke down around mile 19 he jogged to the stadium and crossed the line first. Alice Roosevelt, the President's daughter, crowned him with a wreath and was about to give him the gold when he was exposed. He was banned for life; the ban was cut to six months, and in 1905 he won the Boston Marathon.

The real winner, Thomas Hicks, wanted to lie down; his trainers gave him strychnine (rat poison) mixed with egg white and brandy, more than once. He hallucinated and was carried over the line while his feet kept moving. Fourth place went to Andarín Carvajal, a Cuban mailman who had lost his money gambling, ran in street clothes cut into shorts, ate rotten apples and took a nap. Len Tau was chased about a mile off course by a dog.
