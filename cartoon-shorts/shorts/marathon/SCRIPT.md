# The Olympic Marathon Won by Car

Format: story mode. One event (the 1904 St. Louis Olympic marathon), three absurd runners, one twist ending:
the man who cheated by car was banned for life, the ban lasted six months, and he won the Boston Marathon
the next spring, "on foot, this time".

**Hook (owner's rule):** frame 0 is mid-race. Fred Lorz sits in an open 1904 car, waving at exhausted dusty
runners as it roars past them in a cloud of dust. Horn honk + engine on frame 0; the full claim ("got there
by car") is spoken by about 2.5 s.

**Humour rule:** laugh at the organisers and the absurdity. Hicks nearly died; that is said plainly once
("four doctors") through the visuals, never mocked.

## Facts (2+ sources each)
- 30 Aug 1904, St. Louis (World's Fair Olympics), about 90 °F at the 3 pm start, dusty unpaved country roads. 32 starters, 14 finishers. Sources: Wikipedia "Athletics at the 1904 Summer Olympics – Men's marathon"; The Collector; Smithsonian (2012).
- Chief organiser James E. Sullivan set up only one water source (a well at about 12 miles) on purpose, to study "purposeful dehydration". Sources: Wikipedia; Smithsonian; Today I Found Out.
- Officials rode in cars ahead of the runners and raised dust clouds; William Garcia was found unconscious from the dust. Sources: Wikipedia; The Collector.
- Fred Lorz got cramps after about 9 miles, rode in a car and waved at spectators and runners; the car broke down at about mile 19 and he jogged to the finish, crossing first. Sources: Wikipedia; The Collector; Today I Found Out.
- Alice Roosevelt, the President's daughter, put a wreath on him and was about to give him the gold medal when he was exposed. Sources: Wikipedia; Smithsonian.
- Thomas Hicks wanted to stop and lie down; his trainers refused and gave him strychnine (rat poison) with egg white/raw egg and brandy, more than one dose. He hallucinated and was carried over the line while his feet shuffled in the air. Sources: Wikipedia; The Collector; Smithsonian photo caption.
- Andarín (Félix) Carvajal, a Cuban mailman, lost all his money gambling in New Orleans, ran in street clothes cut into shorts, snatched two peaches, ate bad apples from an orchard, took a nap and still finished fourth. Sources: Wikipedia; The Collector; Today I Found Out. (One historian, Matthews, disputes the apple story.)
- Len Tau was chased about a mile off course by a dog and finished ninth. Sources: Wikipedia; The Collector ("dog attacks").
- Lorz was banned for life by the AAU; it was cut to six months (Feb 1905) and he won the 1905 Boston Marathon. Sources: Wikipedia; Today I Found Out.

Script:
The first man to finish this Olympic marathon...
got there by car.
Saint Louis, nineteen-oh-four. Ninety degrees, on dusty dirt roads.
The man in charge set up just one water stop. On purpose.
He wanted to study dehydration.
Officials drove cars ahead of the runners, so everyone ran in a cloud of dust.
At mile nine, a runner named Fred Lorz got cramps.
So he hopped into a car, and waved at the runners as he drove past them.
Ten miles later, the car broke down. So Fred jogged the rest.
Fred crossed the line first. The crowd went wild.
The President's daughter put a wreath on his head, and almost handed him the gold.
Then someone shouted: he came by car!
The real leader, Thomas Hicks, wanted to lie down. His trainers said no.
They gave him rat poison, mixed with egg whites... and a shot of brandy.
Then another dose.
He started seeing things. His team carried him over the line, with his feet still running in the air.
Fourth place went to a Cuban mailman who had lost all his money gambling.
He ran in his street clothes, cut into shorts.
He snatched two peaches, ate rotten apples from an orchard...
and took a nap. And still came fourth.
Another runner got chased a mile off course by a dog.
And Fred Lorz? He was banned for life. The ban lasted six months.
The next spring, he won the Boston Marathon.
On foot, this time.
