# The Chicken That Lived Without a Head

Format: story mode. One event, one mechanism (the axe spared the jugular and the brain stem that runs the body),
twist/callback ending: the mother-in-law who loved the neck never got it. Hook rule: claim spoken by ~2.3 s, frame 0 =
the headless chicken strutting while the farmer stares, gurgle-crow on frame 0.

## Facts (sources)
- Mike, a Wyandotte rooster, Fruita, Colorado; 10 Sep 1945 farmer Lloyd Olsen chose him for supper. Wikipedia "Mike the Headless Chicken"
- The mother-in-law was visiting and loved the neck, so Lloyd aimed to leave as much neck as possible. miketheheadlesschicken.org (via odditycentral.com, h2g2)
- The axe missed the jugular vein; a clot stopped the bleeding; most of the brain stem survived (breathing, heart rate, reflexes). Wikipedia
- He tried to peck and preen; his crow was a gurgle. Wikipedia
- Fed milk and water with an eyedropper (plus corn, worms). Wikipedia
- Grew from about 2.5 lb to nearly 8 lb. miketheheadlesschicken.org (via odditycentral.com)
- Toured sideshows; 25 cents admission; up to $4,500 a month; Time and Life covered him. Wikipedia
- Copycat beheadings: the longest survivor ("Lucky") lasted 11 days. odditycentral.com
- March 1947, Phoenix motel: he choked on mucus at night; the feeding/cleaning syringes had been left at the sideshow. Wikipedia
- Fruita holds Mike the Headless Chicken Day every year (since 1999), incl. a "Run Like a Headless Chicken" 5K. Wikipedia
- Staging only: the computer/screen analogy; "the mother-in-law never got her neck" (follows from the facts).

## Title
The Chicken That Lived Without a Head

Script:
This rooster lived eighteen months without a head.
Colorado, nineteen forty-five. Farmer Lloyd Olsen picks a rooster for dinner.
His mother-in-law is coming, and she loves the neck.
So Lloyd aims the axe high, to leave her as much neck as possible.
Whack.
And the chicken just walks away.
The next morning, Mike is still walking around. Trying to peck. Trying to crow. With no head.
Wait. How is that even possible?
You see, the axe missed the big vein in his neck, and a blood clot stopped the bleeding.
And it left most of his brain stem, the part that runs breathing, the heartbeat, and walking.
It's like unplugging a computer screen. The screen goes dark, but the computer keeps running.
So Lloyd feeds him milk and water with an eyedropper, straight down the neck.
The rooster keeps on growing, from two and a half pounds, to almost eight.
He goes on tour. People pay twenty-five cents to see him. Life magazine takes his picture.
Lloyd makes four thousand five hundred dollars a month.
Other farmers try to make their own headless chicken. None of them last more than eleven days.
One night, in a motel, Mike starts to choke.
The syringe that clears his throat? Lloyd left it at the show.
Today, his town throws him a party every year. Mike the Headless Chicken Day.
And the mother-in-law? She never got her neck.
