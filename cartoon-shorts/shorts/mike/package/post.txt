The Chicken That Lived 18 Months Without a Head #Shorts

DESCRIPTION:
On 10 September 1945 in Fruita, Colorado, farmer Lloyd Olsen picked a young Wyandotte rooster for supper. His mother-in-law was visiting and loved the neck, so the story goes that he aimed the axe high to leave as much neck as possible. The rooster, Mike, got up and walked away. The axe had missed the jugular vein, a clot stopped the bleeding, and most of the brain stem, which runs breathing, heart rate and reflexes, was left. Mike tried to peck and preen, and his "crow" was a gurgle.

Olsen fed him milk and water with an eyedropper. Mike grew from about 2.5 pounds to nearly 8, toured sideshows at 25 cents a look, was covered by Time and Life, and earned Olsen up to $4,500 a month. Copycat beheadings failed; the longest survivor lasted 11 days. In March 1947, in a Phoenix motel, Mike choked in the night: the syringes used to clear his throat had been left at the sideshow. Fruita has celebrated Mike the Headless Chicken Day every year since 1999.
