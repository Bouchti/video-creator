TITLE (max 100 chars):
The Day Boston Drowned in 2 Million Gallons of Syrup

DESCRIPTION:
On 15 January 1919, a 50-foot steel tank full of molasses burst on Commercial Street in Boston's North End. About 2.3 million gallons poured out as a wave up to 25 feet high, moving at about 35 mph. It knocked a streetcar off its tracks, threw a truck into the harbor and buckled the elevated railway. One boy, Anthony di Stasio, rode the crest of the wave and survived. 21 people died and about 150 were hurt.

The tank had been built under the company's treasurer, Arthur Jell, who had no engineering background and could not read blueprints. It was never properly tested, it leaked from the start, and it was painted brown to hide the leaks. Neighbours collected the leaking molasses to take home. The company blamed anarchists. After three years of hearings, a court-appointed auditor found the company responsible, and it paid $628,000. The harbor stayed brown until summer, and people said the area smelled of molasses for decades. The molasses was used to make alcohol. The very next day, 16 January 1919, the 18th Amendment (Prohibition) was ratified.
