The War Australia Lost to Birds #Shorts

DESCRIPTION:
In November 1932, about 20,000 emus moved onto the wheat farms of Western Australia's Campion district. The farmers, many of them ex-soldiers, asked the Minister of Defence, Sir George Pearce, for help. He sent Major G.P.W. Meredith of the Royal Australian Artillery with two gunners, two Lewis machine guns and 10,000 rounds, plus a Fox Movietone cameraman.

At a dam, more than 1,000 emus walked toward an ambush; the gun jammed after about twelve birds were hit. A gun mounted on a truck couldn't keep up with the birds (emus can sprint at around 50 km/h) and the ride was too rough to aim. The emus split into small groups, each with a tall black-plumed bird keeping watch. After bad press the troops were withdrawn on 8 November, and in Parliament a member asked whether a medal would be struck for the soldiers. They returned, and Meredith reported 986 kills from 9,860 rounds: ten bullets per emu. His verdict: "If we had a military division with the bullet-carrying capacity of these birds it would face any army in the world."
