# The War Australia Lost to Birds

Format: story mode (viral-format research, 2026-11-05). One campaign, one mechanism (a machine gun needs a crowd, emus
scatter into small groups with lookouts), twist ending on Meredith's own quote. No viewer question in the video.

## Facts (sources)
- Nov-Dec 1932, Campion district (Chandler / Walgoolan), Western Australia: ~20,000 emus moved onto the wheat farms
  after breeding; the farmers, many of them ex-soldiers, sent a delegation to Defence Minister Sir George Pearce.
  Wikipedia "Emu War"
- Pearce sent Major G.P.W. Meredith (Royal Australian Artillery), Sergeant McMurray, Gunner O'Halloran, two Lewis guns,
  10,000 rounds. A Fox Movietone cinematographer came along. Wikipedia; straightdope.com
- 4 Nov: over 1,000 emus came toward an ambush at a dam; the gun jammed after 12 birds were killed. Wikipedia
- Meredith mounted a gun on a truck; it could not keep up with the birds and the ride was too rough to fire. Wikipedia
- Emus can sprint at about 50 km/h. Australian Museum "Emu"
- Ornithologist Dominic Serventy: "The machine-gunners' dreams of point-blank fire into serried masses of Emus were soon
  dissipated." Each pack had a leader, "a big black-plumed bird which stands fully six feet high", keeping watch. Wikipedia
- 8 Nov: troops withdrawn after bad press; in Parliament Rowley James mockingly asked whether a medal would be struck
  for the soldiers. Wikipedia
- The troops returned 13 Nov; by 10 Dec Meredith reported 986 kills from 9,860 rounds: ten per bird. Wikipedia
- Meredith: "If we had a military division with the bullet-carrying capacity of these birds it would face any army in
  the world. They can face machine guns with the invulnerability of tanks." Wikipedia (quoting contemporary press)
- Staging only (not claimed as fact): the gunner's "home by lunch" line, the marbles analogy.

## Title
The War Australia Lost to Birds

Script:
In nineteen thirty-two, Australia sent soldiers with machine guns to fight a war against birds. And the birds won.
Twenty thousand emus had marched into the wheat fields of Western Australia, and they were eating everything.
The farmers were old soldiers, so they asked the Minister of Defence for help.
He sent a major, two gunners, two machine guns, and ten thousand bullets.
He even sent a cameraman, to film the victory.
"Just birds, sir? Easy. We'll be home by lunch."
At a farm dam, a thousand birds walked straight toward the guns.
The gun fired, hit twelve birds, and jammed.
So the major put a gun on a truck, to chase them down.
But an emu runs fifty kilometres an hour, and the ride was so bumpy, the gunner couldn't aim at a thing.
Wait, how do birds beat a machine gun?
You see, a machine gun works best on a crowd. And emus refused to stay a crowd.
The moment the shooting started, they split into tiny groups, running in every direction, like a dropped bag of marbles.
And every group had a lookout: a tall black-feathered emu, keeping watch for soldiers.
After a week, the army went home, and in Parliament, someone asked if the soldiers would get a medal.
They came back, and in the end fired nearly ten thousand bullets. That's ten bullets for every emu.
And the major's report? Pure admiration.
"If we had a division with the bullet-carrying capacity of these birds, it would face any army in the world."
