Oregon Blew Up a Whale. On Purpose. #Shorts

DESCRIPTION:
On 9 November 1970 a dead 45-foot sperm whale, about 8 tons, washed up on the beach near Florence, Oregon. Oregon's beaches are legally a state highway, so the job went to the Oregon Highway Division. Engineer George Thornton planned to blow the carcass into pieces small enough for scavengers like seagulls to clean up, using half a ton (20 cases) of dynamite. Walter Umenhofer, a war veteran with explosives training who happened to be there, warned that 20 sticks would be enough. He was ignored.

On 12 November the blast threw blubber up to 800 feet; big chunks came down on the onlookers and their cars. The noise scared the birds away, and most of the whale was still on the beach. Thornton told the Register-Guard: "It went just exactly right." Umenhofer's new car, bought days earlier at a dealer's "Get a Whale of a Deal" promotion, was crushed by a falling chunk of blubber. In 2020 Florence named a park "Exploding Whale Memorial Park".
