# The Jet That Landed on a Drag Race (the Gimli Glider)

Day 6 (13 Nov 2026). Angle chosen by the owner on 12 Nov 2026: "race day + van". Air Canada Flight 143 runs out of
fuel because of a pounds-vs-kilos mistake, glides onto an old air base that is now a drag strip on Family Day, and the
mechanics sent to fix the jet run out of fuel on the way. Topic check (12 Nov): no animated Short found; long-form

## Facts (2+ sources each unless marked)
- 23 July 1983, Air Canada Flight 143, Boeing 767-200 (C-GAUN), Montreal-Ottawa-Edmonton, 61 passengers and 8 crew.
  Sources: Wikipedia "Gimli Glider"; aviation-safety.net ASN 327590; CBC Archives "When a metric mix-up led to the
  'Gimli Glider' emergency".
- The fuel quantity indicator system was faulty, so the gauges were blank; the tanks were measured with drip sticks.
  Sources: Wikipedia; aopa.org "The Gimli Glider" (2000); Damn Interesting "The Gimli Glider".
- Canada was switching to metric; the 767 was Air Canada's first metric aircraft (fuel in kg). The crew used 1.77
  (lb per litre) instead of 0.8 (kg per litre), so the jet carried about 45 % of the fuel it needed. Sources:
  Wikipedia; viewfromthewing.com (2025); amusingplanet.com (2022); bad.technology.
- The check was repeated in Ottawa with the same wrong factor. Sources: Wikipedia; Damn Interesting.
- At 41,000 ft over Red Lake, Ontario, both engines flamed out; a "bong" warning nobody had heard before; most of the
  electronic flight instruments went blank (a ram air turbine gave basic hydraulics). Sources: Wikipedia; Damn
  Interesting; historicwings.com (2012).
- Captain Robert (Bob) Pearson was an experienced glider pilot; First Officer Maurice Quintal had served at RCAF
  Station Gimli. Sources: Wikipedia; migflug.com; CBC.
- Gimli's old runway had become a drag strip; that day was "Family Day" of the Winnipeg Sports Car Club, with race
  cars, families and campers. Sources: Wikipedia; Car Throttle "That Time A Boeing 767 Crash Landed On A Race Track";
  migflug.com.
- Boys on bicycles were on the runway near the touchdown point (two or three, accounts differ; Pearson to CBC: three).
  Sources: CBC "As It Happens"; Car Throttle; medium.com/timeline.
- Too high and fast, Pearson used a forward slip (flying crossed-controls, the jet going sideways) to lose height.
  Sources: Wikipedia; migflug.com; aopa.org.
- The nose gear did not lock (gravity drop) and collapsed; the jet stopped on the strip short of the people; racers
  put out a small nose fire with hand extinguishers; nobody died, minor injuries on the steep rear slide. Sources:
  Wikipedia; historicwings.com; Legion Magazine "The mathematical miracle of the Gimli Glider".
- The mechanics driving from Winnipeg to fix the jet ran out of fuel on the way. Sources: Damn Interesting; Gimli
  Community Web; wadenelson.com; Wikipedia (2006 version). (FLAG: widely repeated, secondary sources only, no 1983
  news report found.)

Script:
No fuel. No engines. Landing on a drag race.
Canada, nineteen eighty-three. A brand-new Air Canada jet... with a broken fuel gauge.
So the ground crew measures the tanks with a dipstick.
Canada just went metric. The new jet counts fuel in kilos. The crew's math used pounds.
So they load less than half the fuel they need.
In Ottawa, they check again. Same mistake.
Forty-one thousand feet. Bong. A warning nobody has ever heard.
Left engine: dead. Right engine: dead. The fancy screens: black.
Lucky for them, Captain Bob Pearson flies gliders. For fun.
His co-pilot knows an old air force base nearby. Gimli.
Nobody told them it's now a drag strip. And today is Family Day.
Race cars. Kids on bikes. And a silent jet, coming right at them.
Too high, so Bob uses a glider trick. He flies the jet sideways.
The nose wheel collapses. The jet scrapes to a stop, just short of the crowd.
Racers put out the fire. Everyone survives.
And the mechanics sent to fix the jet? Their van ran out of fuel on the way.
