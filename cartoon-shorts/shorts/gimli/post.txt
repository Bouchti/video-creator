The Jet That Ran Out of Fuel at 41,000 Feet and Landed on a Drag Race

DESCRIPTION:
On 23 July 1983, Air Canada Flight 143, a brand-new Boeing 767 flying from Montreal to Edmonton via Ottawa with 61 passengers and 8 crew, ran out of fuel at 41,000 feet over Ontario.

The jet's fuel gauges were broken, so the ground crew measured the tanks with drip sticks. Canada was switching to the metric system and the 767 was Air Canada's first aircraft measured in kilograms, but the conversion used 1.77 (pounds per litre) instead of 0.8 (kilograms per litre). The jet took off with less than half the fuel it needed. In Ottawa the tanks were checked again, with the same wrong number.

Over Red Lake, Ontario, both engines stopped and most of the new electronic flight screens went dark. Captain Bob Pearson happened to be an experienced glider pilot. First Officer Maurice Quintal had served at the old RCAF base at Gimli, Manitoba, and steered them there. Nobody in the cockpit knew that one of its runways had become a drag strip, and that day was Family Day for the local sports car club, with race cars, families, campers and kids on bikes on the strip.

Coming in too high, Pearson used a forward slip, a glider pilot's trick of flying the jet crossed-controls, almost sideways, to lose height fast. The nose gear hadn't locked and collapsed on touchdown; the jet scraped to a stop short of the crowd, racers put out a small fire in the nose with hand extinguishers, and nobody died (a few people were bruised on the steep rear slide). The mechanics driving out from Winnipeg to fix the jet reportedly ran out of fuel on the way.
