The Leaning Tower of Pisa Was Saved by a War #Shorts

DESCRIPTION:
In 1173 Pisa started a bell tower in pure white marble. Five years later, at the second floor, one side began sinking into the soft ground. Then Pisa went to war and the builders downed tools for almost a hundred years.

That pause saved it: the clay under the tower had time to squeeze out its water and firm up, like wet sand going hard under your feet. Without it, the tower would almost certainly have toppled. The later floors were built taller on the low side, which is why the tower is actually curved. By 1990 it leaned 5.5 degrees and was closed; engineers first stacked 870 tonnes of lead on the high side, then removed about 70 tonnes of soil from under it, and it settled back by 45 cm. They stopped on purpose.
