The Bribe That Sold the Eiffel Tower #Shorts

DESCRIPTION:
In 1925 the papers in Paris said the Eiffel Tower was rusting and cost a fortune to keep up. It had been built for the 1889 World's Fair and was only meant to stand for twenty years. So con man Victor Lustig had a forger make fake government letterhead, posed as a Deputy Director-General of the Ministry of Posts and Telegraphs, and invited five scrap-metal dealers to the Hôtel de Crillon to bid on the tower, in total secrecy.

He took them on a tour in a limousine and picked the most eager buyer, André Poisson. When Poisson's wife grew suspicious, Lustig "confessed" that his government pay didn't cover his lifestyle. Poisson heard a request for a bribe and relaxed: only a real, corrupt official would ask for one. He paid the bribe and the price, and Lustig left for Austria with the money. Poisson was too embarrassed to go to the police, so about a month later Lustig came back to Paris to sell the tower again.

Would you have paid? 👇
