Why Did This Man Hold a Hoover Van Hostage for 13 Days?

DESCRIPTION:
In August 1992, Hoover's UK business offered two free return flights to anyone who bought at least £100 of Hoover products. From November the flights went to the USA (New York or Orlando). The cheapest qualifying vacuum cost about £119, and the two flights were worth at least £600.

Hoover expected about 50,000 applications and got around 300,000. People bought vacuums they didn't need, the small ads filled up with Hoovers still in their boxes, and the factories went onto seven-day overtime shifts. Claiming was made hard: 14-day deadlines for receipts and forms, and Hoover could refuse the dates and airports customers chose.

Dave Dixon of Workington bought a Hoover washing machine to get the flights. The tickets never came, the machine broke down, and the Hoover technician told him: "If you think buying a washing machine's going to get you two tickets to America, you must be an idiot." In June 1993, Dixon held a Hoover van in his driveway, blocked in by his horse truck, for 13 days, until the High Court ordered its release.

Hoover's owner Maytag fired the president of Hoover Europe and two marketing executives, paid for flights for about 220,000 customers, and in 1995 sold Hoover Europe to the Italian company Candy.
