# The Man Who Took a Hoover Van Hostage

Format (plan, from Day 4): one hero (Dave Dixon of Workington), one mystery (why would a man hold a vacuum
company's van hostage?), a puzzling-action hook, the payoff at the end, and it loops back to the hook.
Angle chosen by the owner on 11 Nov 2026: the van hostage. About 12 Shorts already tell the Hoover flights story
the usual way ("spend £100, get flights, Hoover loses millions"); none is built around Dixon.

**Hook:** frame 0 = the horse truck reversing hard across the driveway, BANG, boxing the white HOOVER van in; Dave
leaps onto the van roof, arms folded, the horse looking out of its box. "This man took a Hoover van hostage. For
thirteen days." spoken by about 2.5 s.

**Humour:** laugh at Hoover's maths and at the absurd escalation, never at anyone's money troubles.
Laugh beats: the horse truck; the small ads full of Hoovers still in the box; the factory on 7-day shifts for
vacuums nobody wanted; the washing machine breaking; the repairman's line; the twist "who was the idiot?".

## Facts (2+ sources each unless marked)
- Aug 1992: Hoover UK offered two free return flights (Europe) for £100+ of Hoover products; from 1 Nov 1992, two
  return flights to the USA (New York or Orlando). Sources: Wikipedia "Hoover free flights promotion"; The Hustle,
  "The worst sales promotion in history" (2024); Green Left Weekly (1993).
- Slogan "Two free flights! Unbelievable!". Sources: Wikipedia; The Hustle (Guardian ad, Oct 1992).
- The cheapest qualifying vacuum was about £119; the two flights were worth at least £600. Sources: The Hustle;
  Expensive Explained Short description citing Chief Marketer "A Bloody Dust Up" (2005).
- Hoover expected about 50,000 applications; about 300,000 came. Sources: Wikipedia; The Hustle.
- Unwanted Hoovers still in their boxes filled the small ads. Sources: Chief Marketer (witness: small ads of the
  Evening Times "full of unwanted Hoovers still in their boxes"); The Hustle ("glut of unwanted, unused secondhand
  Hoovers ... purchased by people just for the free flights").
- Hoover put its factories on seven-day overtime shifts. Sources: The Hustle; Wikipedia ("had to increase their
  production and hire new employees").
- Claiming was made hard: receipts within 14 days, forms within 14 days, Hoover could refuse the customer's
  choices and impose inconvenient flights. Sources: Wikipedia; The Hustle.
- David (Dave) Dixon bought a Hoover washing machine to get the flights; no tickets came; the machine broke down;
  the Hoover technician told him "If you think buying a washing machine's going to get you two tickets to America,
  you must be an idiot." Source: Wikipedia (citing the BBC). (single source for the quote: flagged)
- June 1993, Workington: Dave Dixon, 42, held a Hoover van in his driveway, blocked by his horse truck, for 13 days
  until the High Court ordered its release. Sources: The Hustle; The Guardian, June 1993 (photo caption "Dave
  Dixon with the van he is holding", leader: "the Great Hoover Van of Workington").
- Maytag sacked Hoover Europe's president and two marketing executives (William Foust, Brian Webb, Michael
  Gilbey). Sources: Wikipedia (BBC, 2004); The Hustle; Green Left Weekly (1993).
- Maytag was made to pay for flights for about 220,000 customers. Sources: The Hustle; "pricetag" Short description.
- Hoover Europe was sold to Candy in 1995. Sources: Wikipedia; The Hustle.

## Title options ("Why ...?" + hard number)
1. Why Did This Man Hold a Hoover Van Hostage for 13 Days?
2. Why a Man Blocked a Hoover Van With His Horse Truck for 13 Days
3. Why Hoover's Free Flights Ended With a Van Taken Hostage

Caption question: Who was the real idiot here: Dave, or Hoover? 👇

Script:
This man took a Hoover van hostage.
For thirteen days.
He blocked it in his driveway... with his horse truck.
Why? It started with a free flight.
Britain, nineteen ninety-two. Hoover's deal: spend a hundred pounds, get two free flights. To America.
The cheapest vacuum cost a hundred and nineteen pounds. The flights were worth six hundred.
Hoover expected fifty thousand people. It got three hundred thousand.
People bought vacuums they didn't even need. The small ads filled up with Hoovers... still in the box.
The factory went to seven-day shifts.
So Hoover made claiming hard. Fourteen-day deadlines. Flights nobody wanted.
In Workington, Dave Dixon bought a Hoover washing machine. His tickets never came.
Then the washing machine broke.
The repairman told him:
If you think buying a washing machine's going to get you two tickets to America... you must be an idiot.
So when a Hoover van parked at Dave's house... he blocked it in. With the horse truck.
Thirteen days later, the High Court set the van free.
And Hoover? It fired three bosses, flew two hundred and twenty thousand people, and sold its whole European business.
So... who was the idiot?
