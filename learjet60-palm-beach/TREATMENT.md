# TREATMENT — "Four Minutes over Palm Beach"

Style: Satellite Briefing. 1920×1080, 24 fps, 65 s, English voice + transcript strip, −14 LUFS.
Subject: the crash of Learjet 60 N722BD on 1 November 2026 (FACTS.md). Tone: a calm analyst. No victim names, no theory of cause, no pilot's last words.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **The journey.** Orbit → Austin → across the Gulf → Florida → the dive → the ground. Clear, but the first 20 s are a map lesson before anything happens.
2. **The countdown.** One locked frame over Palm Beach; a clock runs from 12:47:00 to 12:51:50 while radar cells scan in and the altitude counter falls. Strong tension, but no sense of where the jet came from or where it was going, and the camera barely moves.
3. **Ground first, then the fall.** Open on the impact point (the hook), climb to orbit to show the whole trip, come back down at cruise height into the storm, then the camera falls **with** the jet: the altitude readout of the camera is the jet's altitude. End on the ground, then climb out to an open question.

**Chosen: 3.** It hooks on the place in the first second. The dive becomes the signature shot because the style's own instrument (the altitude readout) tells the story, and the climb out says the investigation isn't finished. It borrows the best of 1 (context) and 2 (the clock, during the storm section).

## 2. Logline and arc

At 12:51 on a Sunday, a business jet fell almost straight down into a Florida neighbourhood; the satellite record shows where it came from, the storm it met, and how fast it fell, but not yet why.

- Setup: the ground, then the trip (Austin → Bahamas at 35,000 ft).
- Turn: 12:47, the storm, the request to climb; then the dive: 26,500 ft in about a minute.
- Ending: the site, the displaced neighbourhood, the NTSB's first finding (possible break-up before impact), and the open question: cause unknown.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Open-source investigation films that annotate satellite pictures, and FAA/NTSB animation of flight data.** Learn: one claim per screen, a timestamp on every claim, restraint around loss of life, a source under every number. Don't take: their layouts, logos, type or music.
- **Powers of Ten.** Learn: one continuous change of scale with a running readout. Don't take: its framing or soundtrack.

## 8. Subtitles and titles

- Transcript strip bottom left: 2 px white rule, dark plate fading right, IBM Plex Sans 34 px, one line per cue (long lines split in two cues).
- Reading (accent amber #FFBE55): Barlow Condensed Light 120 px in the right third, label above (Plex Sans 30 px), source note below (Plex Mono 22 px: "FAA, via reports", "NOAA NEXRAD", "measured").
- Instruments: altitude readout (top left) and scale bar (bottom right) in Plex Mono 24 px; imagery credit bottom right ≥ 20 px.
- Title: none at the start (the hook is the place). End card: serif (IBM Plex Serif) "Learjet 60 · N722BD", a line "1 November 2026 · Palm Beach County, Florida", the reading "Cause: under investigation", source "NTSB".
- Colour: grey analyst grade on everything; true colour only at orbit (shot 2–3 and 9). Accent amber for the single reading; the radar outlines in white hairlines with the strongest cores in amber.

## Imagery and data plan (all public domain)

## As built (changes from the plan above)

- Narration start times were fitted to the real Kokoro durations; radar scans moved to half-beats 21.375-23.625 s so the camera
  is already down at 150 km when they print; the break-up note moved to 54.75 s so it can be read.
- The dive reading is the camera's own height, set from the FAA's two reported altitudes (FACTS.md).
- The impact is the loudest moment of the film (momentary −9.3 LUFS), after a 0.35 s suck-out; silence 1 at 30.75-33.75 s
  (the narrator speaks inside it), silence 2 at 45.75-47.6 s.
- Compared with the style's demo: different structure (ground first, then the trip, then the fall), opening (a nadir
  neighbourhood painted in by a scan), signature shot (the camera falling with the jet), camera path, score (80 BPM, one low
  impact) — the end card shares the style's native "reticle becomes the frame" move.
