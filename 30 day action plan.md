## 1. The targets

| Target | What you need | What it pays | When |

**The numbers you must hit for target A:**
- **Views:** 10M Shorts views by 15 Dec ≈ **263,000 views a day**. In practice that means one Short has to break out (1M+), then follow-ups ride it.
- **Subscribers:** 1,000 subscribers ≈ 500,000 views at a normal 2 subs per 1,000 views. Views are the hard part, not subs.
- **Which counter to use:** count "engaged views" (Studio > Analytics > Advanced Mode), not the public counter. The public counter can run about 1.7x higher. **Every number in this plan means engaged views.**

## 2. Every day (the routine)

1. **Check the topic (2 min, before scripting).** Search YouTube Shorts for it. If the top results already have 1M+ views and tell it the same way, find an angle they missed (a lesser-known detail, a different person's view, a number nobody used) or swap the topic.
2. **Make and publish one Short** (60-70 s, story format, hook by 2.5 s, twist ending, question in the caption).
   - **Every story must be very funny and have a very strong hook** (owner's rule, 7 Nov).
   - **Hook:** the most absurd true fact of the story, shown mid-action on frame 0 with a sound hit, full claim spoken by 2.5 s.
   - **Funny:** at least 3 laugh beats built from real facts (an absurd detail, a deadpan line, a visual gag), plus a comic twist ending. Laugh at the absurdity, never at the victims.
   - **Gate before render:** the stills review scores the hook and the humour separately, and both must reach 8/10. If the facts can't carry the jokes, swap the topic.
3. **Human pass (10 min).** The pipeline makes the video; you check it:
   - Write the title and description yourself, with sources listed.
   - Make sure the ending and payoff aren't a repeat of a recent Short.
   - Make sure the look (camera, music, colours, hook type) differs from the last few Shorts.
4. **Title:** event name + a hard number or outcome. Example: "The Town That Blew Up a Whale With 20 Cases of Dynamite".
5. **Post the same clean file** (not a YouTube download) to TikTok, Instagram Reels and Facebook Reels.
6. **Log 24 h and 48 h numbers** (15 min) for each Short:
   - viewed vs swiped away %
   - average view duration
   - engaged views
   - new subs
7. **Reply to the top comments.** If a comment asks a good question, answer it with a reply Short.

**Every Monday:** send 5 client pitches and follow up on last week's (see section 4).

## 3. The 5 numbers and what to do

| Number (per Short) | Bad | Good | If bad... |
|---|---|---|---|
| Viewed vs swiped away | under 60% | 70-90% | 3 of 7 Shorts bad → rebuild the hook: frame 0 mid-action, full claim by 2.5 s, sound hit |
| Average view duration (65 s Short) | under 30 s | 45 s+ | A whole week bad → test 45-55 s cuts on YouTube; keep 61 s+ for TikTok and Facebook |
| Engaged views after 7 days | under 1,300 | 10,000+ | Change the topic type, not the style |
| Subs per 1,000 engaged views | under 1 | 2+ | Stronger ending, a channel trailer, a pinned comment |
| **Breakout:** 100K **engaged** views in 72 h | | | **Drop the calendar.** Make 3-5 follow-ups of the same type within 7 days |

Never delete or re-upload a flop. YouTube can flag that as spam.

## 6. Stay safe from "mass-produced content" rejection

YouTube rejects channels whose videos "feel interchangeable". A fully automatic daily pipeline with one synthetic voice is exactly what that rule looks for, so the variation has to be built in and visible.

**In the pipeline:**
- For each video, pick the camera style, shot count, music cue, colour palette and hook type from a pool. Don't repeat the same combination within 7 days.
- Every Short has its own place, era, props and payoff. The same joke never ends the same way twice.
- Only the intro and outro may repeat.

**By you (the 10-minute human pass):**
- Hand-written title and description, with sources listed.
- Check the ending and the look against the last few Shorts.
- Your own voice on the trailer and the long-video intros.

**AI label:**
- Clearly cartoon-style animation: no label needed.
- 3D renders that could pass for real footage, or a realistic voice of a real person: label it.
- If unsure, label it.

## 7. If the month ends short (the likely case)

- **Expected:** in a typical first month, 30 Shorts at median performance bring 30-80K views and 50-150 subs. Shorts channels usually need about 90 uploads to reach 1,000 subs. At one a day, that's early February.
- **After 1 Feb, the easier route:** 8,000 long-form watch hours in a year, about 11,500 long-video views a month. Keep daily Shorts for discovery and add one long video a week.
- **What long videos pay:** $15-18 per 1,000 views is the **best case** (education channels). Weird-history stories may be classed as entertainment, which pays about $1-5. So that route is worth roughly **$150-$2,500 a year** at 11,500 views a month. Shorts pay about $0.05-0.30 per 1,000.
- **So the real money for the next few months is client work.** Keep the weekly pitches going whatever happens on YouTube.
- **Rejected:** appeal within 21 days (faster than the 30-day reapply).
